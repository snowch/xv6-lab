A sleeping lock can stay held across blocking work

Observe process ownership while its internal spinlock is released.

A buffer may stay locked while disk I/O blocks. Holding an ordinary spinlock throughout that wait would violate sched’s contract and waste a CPU on a multiprocessor. A sleeping lock instead records its owning process and puts contenders to sleep.

Its internal spinlock protects the locked flag and PID only while changing or inspecting them. acquiresleep releases that spinlock before returning, while the logical sleeping lock remains owned. releasesleep clears ownership and wakes contenders, which recheck the condition in a while loop.

The probe separately reports ownership of the sleeping lock and of its internal spinlock. This uncontended probe inspects the protocol; the source shows how a contender blocks and rechecks.

C reminder: pointers and the -> operator

For a pointer p to a struct, p->pid means (*p).pid: follow the pointer, then read its pid member. Use the dot operator with a struct value itself. Reading a member and assigning to it are different operations.

p->pid        // read a member through a pointer
p->pid = 7;   // assign a new member value
